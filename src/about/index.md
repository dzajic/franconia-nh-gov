  - id: profile
    title: The Old Man of the Mountain
    icon: ledges
    paragraphs:
      - Five granite ledges on Cannon Mountain lined up to form a stone profile high above Profile Lake. It became the symbol of New Hampshire, on the state quarter and highway signs.
      - On the morning of <strong>May 3, 2003</strong> it fell. Granite and weather give and take; the memory of it is part of the town's story.
  - id: cannon
    title: Cannon Mountain
    icon: peak
    paragraphs:
      - Cannon rises over Franconia Notch. The aerial tramway climbs to the summit, and the mountain is central to the area's ski history and to today's visitors.
      - Skiers, hikers and families all start here.
  - id: ridge
    title: Franconia Ridge
    icon: ridge
    paragraphs:
      - Mount Lafayette, at 5,260 feet, anchors the ridge, with Lincoln and Little Haystack alongside. The walk along it runs above treeline for miles.
  - id: furnace
    title: The Iron Furnace
    icon: furnace
    paragraphs:
      - The octagonal granite stack beside the Gale River is the last surviving blast furnace in New Hampshire. Charcoal from local woods fed it.
      - The Heritage Museum tells the story of how iron built the town.
  - id: forest
    title: Town & Forest
    style: sign
    paragraphs:
      - Much of the land around Franconia is part of the White Mountain National Forest. That is a gift and a responsibility: the forest shapes how the town lives, works and welcomes visitors.
      - A town website should feel like a good trailhead: clearly marked, easy to read, honest about where things are. That is why this design uses spruce, bark and cream, and plain trail-sign labels. It does not use any official Forest Service logo.
