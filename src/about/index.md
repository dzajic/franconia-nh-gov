  - id: history
    title: A short history
    icon: furnace
    paragraphs:
      - "The octagonal stone stack on the far bank of the Gale River is the only blast furnace still standing in New Hampshire. An 1805 map shows an iron smelter on the site. The New Hampshire Iron Factory Company rebuilt it, added a hot blast after 1840, and raised the stack to 32 feet. A stone in the west arch reads “S. Pettee, Jr. 1859.” Farmers burned wood for charcoal to fire it. Production had declined by 1865, and the shed around the stack burned in 1884."
      - "The stack is on private property, and trespassing is prohibited. The interpretive center is kept by the same volunteers who look after the Heritage Museum. The longer account is on the <a href=\"/iron-furnace/\">iron furnace</a> page. The <a href=\"/heritage-museum/\">Heritage Museum</a> page describes an 1878 farmhouse that opened as a museum in 1998, beginning with town historian Sarah Nelson Welch’s collection, and says the collection now holds almost 10,000 items."
