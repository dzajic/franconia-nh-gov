# Principles

Measure a change to the Franconia, New Hampshire town site against this document before it merges.

## Mission

This is a modern, trustworthy, easy-to-maintain website for the people of Franconia, New Hampshire: residents, visitors, and town staff. Boards own their pages. Changes are reviewed in the open and keep a public history. The site stays free, or nearly free, to run.

## Audience

Many readers are older residents with weaker eyesight, so type has to be clear without being oversized. People arrive on a phone and on a desktop. Town staff who update a page are not technical. Write and design for those three.

## Principles

### Business first, delight second

A seasonal theme and photographs may add character. They never compete with a civic task.

So we… cover the decoration and check that paying a bill, finding a form, or opening the minutes is still the obvious next step.

### Readable, not oversized

Desktop type defaults to 15px. A− / A / A+ is about 14 / 15 / 17px on desktop, and 15 / 16 / 17px on a phone, where the default is 16px. Body text stays at least 14px, with generous line height, at WCAG AA contrast.

So we… check the root size, the three text-size steps, the 14px floor, line height near 1.55, and contrast of text on its background.

### The whole card or row is the tap target

Tappable cards and rows are at least 44px, with a subtle hover and press. Underlines are only for inline links in body prose.

So we… tap the card, not a small word inside it, and we look for an underline on anything that is not a link in a paragraph.

### Words stay whole

Hyphenation stays off, and a word does not break in the middle. A long URL or email address may break.

So we… scan for a word split across lines. Only a long URL or email is allowed to break.

### Navigation does not take a fixed slice of the screen

The full header is not sticky. Links that do not fit collapse into a Menu button.

So we… scroll, and confirm the tall header leaves with the page, and that extra links are behind Menu instead of a bar that eats the screen.

### Keep this visual design

The site keeps its trail-sign headers, cream paper, spruce and bark, and ridge footer. It stays a town website.

So we… compare the page with the current site. A new visual language, a dashboard, or an app shell does not belong.

### Section headers are uppercase

Section headers read as trail signs: short, heavy, and in capitals.

So we… read a new heading out loud. It should still sound like a sign, not a sentence-case app title.

### Homepage sections stay short

A homepage section is a short list and ends with one See all link. It does not repeat a link.

So we… count the ways out of the section. One link leads to the full list, and nothing else in that section points at the same place.

### A wide screen uses its width

Desktop layouts put tasks, events, departments, and directory groups side by side. Paragraphs stay a comfortable line length.

So we… widen the window and look for a single skinny column sitting in empty space. Prose still wraps before the line is hard to track.

### Pages stay light

The seasonal theme is about 9KB, and it stays a small addition. The page does not take on a framework or a font file for a visual effect.

So we… treat a new library, webfont, or large script as a failed check unless the page is still this light.

### Every page invites a correction

Every page offers Suggest a change.

So we… find that invitation on the page we touched.

### One change per pull request

Each distinct change is its own pull request, with a preview link and a short description.

So we… split unrelated edits, keep the description to a few bullets, and include the preview link.

## How to evaluate a change

1. Can a resident still finish the civic task on a phone, with the decoration ignored?
2. Is readable text at least 14px, with generous line height, and does contrast still meet WCAG AA?
3. Is a new tap target the whole card or row, at least 44px, with a quiet hover and press and no stray underline?
4. Does any word break in the middle, other than a long URL or email?
5. Does the full header scroll away, with overflow links behind Menu?
6. Does the page still look like this town site, with uppercase section headers?
7. On the homepage, is the section short, with one See all link and no duplicate link?
8. Is this one distinct change, described in a few bullets, with a preview link, and is the page still light?
